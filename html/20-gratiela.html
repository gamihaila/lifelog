Grațiela

The first time Grațiela and I met in Laura’s apartment, neither of us thought much of the encounter. Perhaps because it was a set-up, we both felt awkward, and didn’t even exchange phone numbers or anything. I do remember though Grațiela mentioned something about the Welcome Party at the International Student Center and I tried to dissuade her from going, maybe subconsciously trying to prevent her from meeting somebody else. I knew from my own experience how eager the older students were to meet the newcomers.

So a few weeks passed uneventfully, the new school year started, and I was wrapping up my dissertation: after the SRC internship I was finally looking forward to graduating and getting a real job, something I’d been blissfully putting off during graduate school. Then something happened which changed everything, although I had no idea at the time: an old friend of mine from the Romanian Students’ Club at U of T, Marica, called me and asked if I could help her move to a new apartment. I’m not very athletic, but I figured I could lend a hand, it’s one of the things friends are for, right? So I agreed and went to the address she gave me. She was already there and was unloading a truck with a bunch of Romanian friends as well as a Czech mover, who owned the moving truck. To my surprise, who should I see there among the Romanian friends, you guessed it, Grațiela! It turns out they had met through a U of T mentorship program to which they had both independently signed up, and they had become friends. Grațiela was looking radiant this time, looking on her side and smiling. I followed her glance and saw the Czech mover was also smiling shyly in her direction! Oh, no, I can’t let that happen, I thought to myself, so I started talking to her, keeping her away from the Czech guy. This time we did exchange phone numbers before leaving Marica’s place.

So, not too long after that I called her. She had an uncanny ability to have meaningful phone conversations, and I was completely at ease talking to her, probably because she also enjoyed talking to me. I told her all about my failed marriage (which she knew about from Laura), and about my relationship with Lara. We were just being friendly with each other and I didn’t feel any need to hide anything from her or to embellish my story. Somehow, I gained her trust and she agreed to meet in a park.

When we met, the first thing she wanted to get out of the way was to establish that this was not a date, since I had a girlfriend already. “Well”, I said, “it actually is a date. Me and Lara already established we don’t have a future together. It’s true that we’ve been together for three years, but she made it quite clear that she’s not the marrying kind, and that I should look for someone else”. “OK”, Grațiela said, “I understand that’s how she feels, but how do you feel?” I didn’t quite know what to say to that, my feelings were complicated: I was still feeling affection for Lara, but I was ready to move on. It was honestly strange: me and Lara had not officially broken up, we didn’t have a fight, and we had not been unfaithful to one another. It was just a slowly dying relationship. So I tried my best to explain the situation to Grațiela, who was surprisingly understanding and didn’t run away hearing about all this, like all the other girls I had met while being with Lara. So we continued our date, and kept talking. I remember I was going on and on about how I believed the chances of people meeting a suitable partner were so small that they could be considered negligible. She listened patiently to all my nonsense while we were walking, until we got hungry and we went to eat chicken wings at a Hard Rock Cafe downtown. I offered her a Coke, and she laughed “What Coke, get me a beer if you want to get anything!”. I laughed too and ordered two beers. We had a great time, I was completely at ease, not trying to impress her, and she was also very sincere and open, not trying to play hard to get at all.

After this first date, we started seeing each other regularly, and talking every day on the phone. Naturally, I told Lara about it. First, she didn’t really pay much attention to this, it wasn’t the first time I had told her I met someone else. When she understood that I was being serious, she adopted her tried and true sabotage techniques, and was stunned that they had no effect on Grațiela. You see, Grațiela was already fully aware of the nature of my relationship with Lara. It was as if she had been inoculated and was now immune to Lara’s attempts to undermine our relationship! It didn’t matter what Lara said to her: she even tried to lie that I was still secretly seeing her. Grațiela didn’t care. One night, when Grațiela was visiting, Lara came to the door and started wailing and scratching the door calling for me. “We should open the door, I’m afraid for her”, I kept telling Grațiela. “Don’t worry”, she said, “she’s not going to kill herself”. She was right. After a while, Lara gave up and went home.

However, in the following weeks Lara intensified her campaign and managed to sow enough doubt in Grațiela’s mind to cause her to give me an ultimatum: “George, you need to make up your mind”, Grațiela said to me over the phone one day, “and until you settle things with Lara, it would be best if you stopped calling me”. So, painful as this was, I did stop calling. I wasn’t seeing Lara at the time anymore either, I just needed time to clear my thoughts and I didn’t want to cause Grațiela any more distress. One complicating factor was that before meeting Grațiela I had bought plane tickets for me and Lara to go introduce her to my parents in Bucharest in the summer of 2000. We were now a month away from that trip. Obviously, after everything that happened I was no longer planning to take Lara with me, but knowing her, I wasn’t completely ruling out her showing up at the airport without telling me. Her name was in the airline reservation system and she could board without a physical ticket, after all. Grațiela knew that too and had bought a ticket on the same flight when we started going out. But now, we were not talking anymore, at her request.

So, I went to the airport by myself, bracing for all possibilities. I met Grațiela in the waiting area by the gate, and she was keeping her composure, determined to avoid me. Luckily, Lara had listened to reason and didn’t show up, after all. She was impulsive, but not stupid: what was she going to do in Bucharest by herself if she wasn’t my girlfriend anymore? So me and Grațiela boarded the plane together but didn’t talk much. We stopped over in Amsterdam. We decided it was silly to keep avoiding each other, so we had breakfast together at Schiphol Airport at a place whimsically called “Sandwich Island”. Once we arrived at the Otopeni Airport in Bucharest we parted ways, and she reiterated her request to not call her. Her parents were waiting for her and took her home to her hometown, Alexandria, a couple hours away from the capital. I took a cab and went home to my parents’ apartment in Drumul Taberei.

After the joy of getting reunited with my family, it hit me: I was alone. All my plans were up in the air. I didn’t bring any girl with me to meet my parents, as I had promised.
