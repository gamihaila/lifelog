Tulemod, Inc.

Sometime around this time Grațiela and I met with Dana, who was now living in Palo Alto, just a few streets away from us, in fact. We went together for dinner at a Japanese restaurant, at Dana’s suggestion. At that dinner, I was telling Dana how much fun I was having at Google implementing high performance systems at scale. Everything was going great professionally. The only slight problem was that three years after we sold our house in Yorktown Heights at a loss, we were still renting, and we couldn’t see how we could ever buy another house at the rate Bay Area real estate prices were going. Grațiela told Dana how her mom was constantly on my case about how we gave up that five-bedroom house in Yorktown Heights, New York and we were now all sharing a three-bedroom rental unit in Palo Alto, and lost over one hundred thousand dollars in the process. Hearing this, Dana offered a way out:

“Listen, Grațiela, I’ve just founded a technology start-up in the online fashion retail space, and I’m looking for a founding engineer. I think George would be great for this role, if he’s interested. Of course, he would need to eventually leave Google for this, but we could work out some arrangement where he could initially work part-time alongside his job.”

Now, you need to understand that although I had a very stable position at Google, working on genuinely exciting projects, I had worked at the exact same engineering level I was hired at for the past five years, with no promotion in sight. But, since this was Dana and she already had a successful startup before this, I felt compelled to give this idea a chance.

So, one Saturday afternoon I went to Dana’s place for a meeting with her and her co-founder, another Romanian engineer, named Bogdan. They explained their product idea to me: they were going to develop technology for building a size-accurate three-dimensional model of one’s body shape, reconstructing it from several iPhone selfies; then, they would use this 3D model to generate realistic simulations of how a garment would look and feel on that person. They were going to use this technology in a high-end e-commerce platform that would enable consumers to virtually “try-on” garments from home.

“This sounds nice, but what’s the business idea, who would pay for all this technology?”, I asked Dana.

“No, you don’t understand, George”, she said, “That’s just eye-candy, to get the consumers to interact with the website. The real value is in on-demand manufacturing, reducing the industrial waste generated today by manufacturing all these garments for all possible sizes, a large percentage of which end up in landfill today.”

“Okay, I see, but where do I fit in? I know nothing about fashion”, I said.

“Well, we need someone like you to architect the backend which will collect the website analytics from logs, that we could then sell to advertisers”, clarified Dana.

“I see. Let me think about it. Did you already build the 3D body reconstruction technology? Who’s working on that?”, I asked.

“Oh, that’s me”, intervened Bogdan, “By the way, George, I think we used to be related: I’m Ioana’s cousin from Cluj”

“Oh, that’s why you seemed familiar”, I remembered suddenly, “We came to your home after we got married, around 1990, you have a younger sister, right?”

“That’s right!”, said Bogdan.

“Small world!”, said Dana, “OK, so George, what do you say, are you in?”

“Well, what you’re describing cannot be built by one engineer in his spare time”, I said.

“Oh, no, of course not: we fully expect you to quit your Google job in a few months”, said Bogdan.

“I’ll get back to you”, I said, and went home. Dana gave me access to the corporate AWS account and asked me to poke around and see what it would take to prototype a database on their MongoDB subscription.

I was excited about this new challenge. I even went to the Apple Store the next morning and bought a new MacBook Air with my own money for this. I figured I should not use the Google-issued MacBook Pro for a side job, it was specifically spelled out in my employment contract, I remembered very well. I needed a personal computer anyway, I was telling myself to justify the impulse buy.

That weekend I had to drive Maria to a colorguard competition in Livermore, and I brought my new MacBook Air with me. I stayed in my Nissan Leaf in the Livermore community college parking lot the whole afternoon: I logged into the AWS account and did some initial investigation while waiting for my daughter to be done with her competition. There wasn’t much content there, but I did manage to get some idea about what that project would take.

The next Saturday afternoon I went to Dana’s place again to discuss the conditions. Going in, I was already having an uneasy feeling, thinking how a potential business engagement would alter our friendship irrevocably. But I wanted to give this opportunity a fair chance, and didn’t want to be ungrateful to Dana for offering me this position.

“So, Dana, I did some poking around your AWS account, and I have a better idea about the project now. I would like to hear from you, though, what kind of arrangement do you have in mind, for our collaboration, in the current stage, while I’m still at Google”, I said.

“Well, I was thinking of a part-time contract of ten billable hours a week. Some weeks it may be more, some other weeks it would be less, it would be built around your schedule”, said Dana. “We would pay you a fixed $10K/month for this”

I wasn’t comfortable with that: who knows what Dana thinks could be done in ten hours, what if it would take me twenty or thirty, and it would still not be done to Dana’s satisfaction? And what if there will be tight deadlines for investor presentations? How would I squeeze that extra time around my full-time job at Google?

“Dana, thank you for the offer, but I’m afraid I’m not ready to make this kind of commitment. Sorry, but I will have to pass.”, I said.

“All right, no hard feelings”, said Dana, and that was that. I never looked back on that decision. We remained friends, of course, and I was relieved that we could keep our friendship free of business relationships.

I gave the new MacBook Air to Maria for her birthday.
