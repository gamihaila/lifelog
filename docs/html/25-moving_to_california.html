Moving to California

Deciding to move was easy, actually doing it was harder than I thought. First of all, while it was true that internal transfers were possible, they weren’t as easy as just asking to move. One had to find a team to join in the other office. To facilitate that, there was an internal website where managers could post open positions for internal transfers. I spent weeks scouring those listings, and got some calls, but none of them materialized into an offer. I needed to expand my search. It so happens that around that time I got contacted by a recruiter from Netflix for a job opening in Los Gatos. I wasn’t really looking to leave Google, but I figured an interview in California could be fun. Especially if I could combine the trip with a visit to the Google office.

I told my manager I’m going to California to visit a friend (which was true, we visited Roxana too), and while I’m there I could meet with the researchers we were working with for our project. So, one rainy day in early May 2012, I took Grațiela and our nine-month-old daughter Xenia with me to Mountain View to soft launch my California dream! We stayed at a nice hotel and while I was interviewing at Netflix or at the office, they explored the downtown area on foot. The Netflix interview didn’t work out, but my visit to the Google Research office paid off: I got to meet the researchers that designed that great question answering system we integrated into Google Search. We talked at length about ways we could improve the system, new scoring signals we could use, how to add normalization, how to account for missing features, and other ideas. The net result was that when their manager visited the New York office a few weeks later he asked to meet with me. My manager had also reached out to him to let him know I am looking for opportunities in the Mountain View office. The way he put it to me was: “If life hands you lemons, make lemonade: I don’t want to lose you, but if you’re going to move to California, at least we can continue to work together in the same project.” Separately, Alon Halevy also visited the New York office and we had coffee together, and I mentioned to him that I would love to transfer to that team. He was a research director then, in a close area, and knew the question answering people well, they were working in the same building. I don’t know what he told his peer about me, but the net result was that my transfer was fast-tracked! We set my move day for August 15.

We packed all our belongings into a moving container, and then all six of us got in our Toyota Highlander and took Highway 80 to the West Coast! We took our time, never driving more than eight hours each day and got there in a week, checking into hotels along the way whenever we got tired. Our older daughters had a blast, they were already used to long road trips from our yearly drives to Florida. The youngest one got a lot of sleep, so she was fine too. In fact, she turned one halfway through: we had a mini birthday party for her when we stopped for lunch. When we got to Utah, in the mountains, we almost couldn’t find a room; it was packed with tourists.

The next day we got back in the car, drove for a while, and just when we were in the middle of the Bonneville Salt Flats, Xenia choked on something and started turning blue. Grațiela panicked and kept asking me to go to the ER, except there was nothing around us for miles. Luckily, she coughed it out and turned pink again!

We finally arrived in Milpitas, California, where I had found a four-bedroom house to rent. We got there before the shipping container (yes, just like in “Inside Out”), and had to sleep on inflatable mattresses for the first few days. Why Milpitas, you ask? Well, on the map it looked like it was pretty close to Mountain View and it was the only place we could afford for a house of that size. We had sold our Yorktown Heights house at a loss (we had paid $415,000 in 2003, and we sold it for $299,000 in 2012, a few years after the sub-prime mortgage crash).

We enjoyed our stay in Milpitas, especially the inexpensive and delicious Asian food there, but pretty soon we realized that the schools were not that great, despite being reasonably well rated on greatschools.org. The stress caused by moving the kids to a worse school was weighing on me pretty heavily. One day, out of the blue, after dropping Maria off at her ballet class with Grațiela, as we walked out on the sidewalk, I felt an excruciating pain in the back of my neck. It was so bad, I crouched down in shock. It felt as if someone stabbed me, a sharp, intense pain, unlike anything I ever experienced before. Luckily it went away after a few minutes so I was able to get up and walk home, but in the subsequent days it started to show up every time I would bend my head forward, for example while brushing my teeth. Naturally I went to see a doctor and she said it’s probably a minor herniated disk and recommended bed rest. I took two weeks’ medical leave from work and lay in bed most of the time. At its worst, it kept me up at night, but I learned how to lie on my right side with my left arm lifted above my head to reduce the pressure. I was very lucky that at Maria’s school we befriended one of her schoolmates’ mothers named Tenny, a medical doctor originally from Vietnam. She prescribed me a low dose of cortisol orally and taught me how to taper it off. Also, she cooked the most delicious Vietnamese spring rolls and brought them to me with a homemade peanut sauce, explaining how this was a low inflammatory meal. Between the cortisol and the spring rolls, I got better, well enough to go back to work. At work I signed up for physical therapy on-site: Google has a state-of-the-art wellness center and the therapists are first class. I also went to get an MRI at Stanford and made an appointment with a Romanian doctor there, a family connection, in a few weeks’ time (he was fully booked).

As I was waiting for my appointment, we found out at church about a visiting monk from Greece that was coming to San Francisco with a cross that many people claimed had healing powers. People would go and wait in long lines for him, and he would place the cross over their body and pray for their health. As a man of science I could not believe in miracles, but Grațiela insisted and I didn’t want to argue with her. Since I met her I attended church services every Sunday with her and our daughters and also read C.S. Lewis’s books on Christianity which appealed to my logical mind, so I couldn’t rule out divine intervention. Besides, what did I have to lose? So I decided to go wait in line for this Greek monk. When my turn came, I realized he didn’t speak any English so I couldn’t tell him what hurt. Regardless, he proceeded to rub his cross over my back and then something unexplainable happened: when he reached the back of my neck, he stopped and it felt like the cross wouldn’t slide, as if it got stuck. Grațiela, who was right behind me in line, later told me she saw the cross stick to my neck without falling. He kept it there for what felt like a full minute, all the while praying in Greek. I didn’t think much about it at the time, and there was no sudden change in my condition. However, over the next few days, whatever was left of my pain subsided completely. Finally, the time came for my Stanford appointment with Dr. Mindea, a renowned specialist. He received me in his office, together with a colleague, and asked to see my MRI. They both looked at it and agreed that they could clearly see the herniated disk. His colleague recommended surgery based on this. Dr. Mindea agreed with his diagnosis, but first wanted to hear from me how bad my pain was. According to the imagery, I should have been in serious pain, he said.

“You know, doctor, I really was in terrible pain but now I’m not. In fact, I haven’t had any pain for weeks.”, I said.

“In this case, I would advise against surgery. You would never be the same if we cut any part of your vertebrae. If it doesn’t hurt, leave it alone. Come back if that changes.”, Dr. Mindea said.

“But doctor, look at his MRI, this is really bad”, the other doctor said.

“Yes, I can see that, but since nothing hurts now, let’s leave it alone”, he said.

I’m not going to say here what made me well. I don’t know. I had cortisol treatment, which helped a lot. I had weeks of physical therapy and exercises with rubber bands which strengthened my muscles. I even had a neck stretching device from the wellness center that looked like a torture implement but did relieve the pain. I’m just relating what happened and letting you draw your own conclusions. All I know is that I’m healthy now and have never had that pain come back again.

…

After a few months of living in Milpitas, Grațiela and I decided we needed to move to a better school district for our kids. So we went back online to look for houses to rent. Eventually we found a three-bedroom townhouse at the southern edge of Palo Alto for under $4,000 a month. The catch: absolutely no maintenance in the rental contract; whatever broke, we would be responsible for fixing it ourselves. The place was also significantly smaller than the Milpitas home, but it had a large back yard; most importantly, though, it allowed our kids to enroll in Palo Alto’s public schools, widely considered one of the best school districts in the country. So, only six months after we moved to Milpitas, we moved again. We had to pay a penalty for breaking the one-year lease early, but it was worth it: we got our daughters into good schools and my commute became walking distance! I actually started walking or cycling to the office almost every day, an amazing improvement after the two-hour commute to the New York office!
