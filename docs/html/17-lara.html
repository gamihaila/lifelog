Single in Toronto

It was fall of 1996. I had just arrived back in Toronto from France and was getting ready for the new school year. One of the activities was an orientation session at the International Student Center. I had not attended in the past but now, as a fresh single, I was determined to get out there and meet new people. OK, I was looking to meet girls. After the new experiences in Paris I was more emboldened to approach strangers and make acquaintances. It was the ideal environment for it too, since everyone there was a new student, typically from another country, and they were therefore open to making new friends. As a somewhat senior student I had stuff to talk about with the newcomers. That helped a lot, as I was normally rather shy. So, as I was walking around I saw a good looking young girl smiling in my direction and I took that as a clue to go talk to her. Something in her physiognomy seemed familiar to me, almost as she was Romanian (I would find out later that her ancestors came from Eastern Europe). She had a beautiful and sincere smile that drew me in right away. As this was the International Students Center, I had the perfect opening:

“Hi, my name is George! I’m from Romania, where are you from?”

“I’m Jenny”, she said, “I’m from right here in Toronto!”

“Oh, so if you’re not an international student, what are you doing here?”, I joked.

“Well, Canada is a nation too”, she laughed, which made her even prettier.

After quite a bit of chit-chat about our majors, and life at the U of T, I thought what a pity it would be if I never saw her again, so I asked her for her e-mail address. You see, I didn’t want to seem too forward by asking for her phone number.

“Oh, sure, here it is”, she said, and dictated an America Online email address to me, starting with “harold”.

“Harold?”, I asked, confused.

“Oh, yeah, it’s my father’s. He taught me to give out his address to anybody who asks me”, she clarified.

“I see, smart!”, I replied, but in my mind I knew I wouldn’t ever email her dad.

Luckily, the U of T campus is not that large, and I bumped into her several times after that, and each time she was friendly, until eventually we did exchange phone numbers and occasionally visited each other.

One time, she called me to ask me if I wanted to go with her to a varsity hockey game she had to write an article about for the university newspaper.

“Great, I’ll bring my camcorder”, I offered, “and I can film it for you so you can go back and write about the more interesting parts.”

“Nah, no need, I’ll just watch it and take notes as I go”, she declined.

Nevertheless, we did watch the game together, and I was happy to be next to her, even if I didn’t care for hockey. After the game, I wanted to take her hand, but she said no, and I didn’t insist.

A month later, on Canadian Thanksgiving, an Italian girl I met during the same orientation party invited me to go with her and some other friends to a Thanksgiving dinner at a Catholic church. I wasn’t a churchgoer at the time so I was reluctant to go but she insisted and the meal was just $5 so I decided to go.

At that meal I saw someone at the other end of the large dinner hall who looked familiar. Thinking it was Jenny I waved to her and she smiled and waved back. I got up from our table and started walking towards her. As I got closer I realized it was not Jenny, but she continued to smile looking at me. When I got there I introduced myself and apologized for my mistake.

“No worries! I’m Lara, by the way,” she said, and continued chatting with me. “Nice meeting you, I’ll see you around,” I said after a few minutes of chitchat.

She mentioned casually she went to the campus gym on Tuesdays and Thursdays at 10. Naturally I went there too the next Tuesday and sure enough I saw her there but just nodded to her, as she was working out on a treadmill. To my surprise she came over to me after her workout and gave me her phone number.

I called her the next day and invited her to see a play that I had already seen before1. It was a musical comedy adaptation by a local troupe. I offered to come pick her up from her home and she started giving me driving directions.

“I don’t have a car,” I said, embarrassed. “Oh, no worries, I live right near a subway stop, and it’s anyway easier to go by transit.”

I was relieved. So I went to her house, and we went to see the play together. During the play I took her hand. She didn’t pull back. I was on cloud nine! I walked her home afterwards and as I was saying my goodbyes, she invited me in. She lived by herself in a large mansion close to the Cedarvale Ravine, complete with a large indoor stairway and jacuzzi. Seeing my surprised face, she clarified she was housesitting for a wealthy friend who just bought this house as an investment property but didn’t rent it out yet.

She cooked a pasta dish for us, and we ate it sitting on the floor. All the time she kept talking and asking me about my life. She was an amazing listener. After dinner she played Debussy for me on her grand piano, beautifully. I was immediately and thoroughly caught under her spell!

Late at night, in her bedroom, we heard noises at the door. Lara jumped up and said it was someone she used to go out with and that I should better leave by the back door, because otherwise this guy would “go berserk”. She needed to handle him by herself, she said. There was nothing I could do but comply. I left through some back streets in the middle of the night, always looking back to make sure I wasn’t followed. Luckily, no one was after me, but I was confused and scared, not knowing what Lara’s situation was, and thinking it was probably not going to work out between us.

To my surprise, though, the next day Lara came over and told me she had indeed met that guy and told him again it was over between them. We started dating right away and a few weeks later I asked her to move in with me and she agreed. Her house sitting arrangement was temporary anyway. Actually, the wealthy friend that owned the house, she clarified, was an ex-boyfriend of hers, called John. She and John had been together for over five years, and were still friends now.

And indeed, a few weeks later, John invited both of us to a party he threw at his home for his employees (he owned a profitable business, with about thirty employees), so we went. It was a sumptuous party, with a chef cooking naan bread in the backyard in a clay oven over an open fire, and an extensive array of hors d’oeuvres and drinks. John was a good natured, generous guy, and he was surprisingly friendly to me, under the circumstances. It was as if he wanted to make sure I will take good care of Lara, of whom he was obviously still very fond of. I could tell by the way he was talking to her that they had a long history together, but John had since moved on, and was now engaged with another woman. At that party, I remember his employees were talking openly among themselves about how they hated the immigrants that kept coming and taking their jobs, quite the opposite of the official governmental line of a “multicultural society”. I mentioned to them I was an immigrant too, but that didn’t make any difference. I remember John intervened and changed the subject to his favorite businessman, Donald Trump. “Mark my words, this man will one day become the president of the United States”, he kept saying. This was in 1996.

After the party, John invited us again to his home a few times, every time a perfect host. I never found out exactly why he and Lara broke up, but I was impressed how they remained such close friends.

Soon after Lara moved in with me, she asked me if I still keep in touch with Ioana and if it would be OK if she called her. I gave her Ioana’s phone number and she called her from my phone:

“Hello, is this Ioana?”, she opened.

“Yes, who is this?”, answered Ioana.

“Hi, you don’t know me, but I do. My name is Lara and I’m dating your ex-husband George”, she introduced herself.

“Oh?”

“Do you mind if I ask you a few questions about George?”

“Not at all, go ahead”, agreed Ioana, taken by surprise.

“Listen”, said Lara, “he asked me to marry him on our first date”.

“Oh, yes”, Ioana answered, “he did the same with me. He totally means it, you know, that’s just how he was raised”.

“I see”, said Lara, “I didn’t know what to think about that, we had just met”.

“No, no, he’s serious. He knows right away.”, Ioana assured her.

“So, listen”, continued Lara, “you left some of your nightgowns in the closet, do you want them back?”

“No”, answered Ioana surprised.

“Then I’ll wear them, they are really comfy! And thanks for George as well, he’s really nice to me!”

“You’re welcome!”, laughed Ioana.

If this conversation sounds surreal to you, you’re not alone. At the time, still fairly new to the country I thought this must be normal, like a new employer asking for references from your past employers. I’m sure Ioana had a good laugh too. Not too late after this conversation they met each other in person at a friend’s wedding we were both invited (actually it was me and Lara who introduced the groom, Ovidiu, to Lara’s best friend Ottilie and they got married, imagine that!). Lara made an extra effort to gaze lovingly into my eyes while slow dancing making sure Ioana can see us on the dance floor. Then she went to her and thanked her again for the nightgowns and for the boyfriend (!). She was like that, never letting a funny situation go unexploited.

We were together for three years. It wasn’t a smooth ride though; she moved out and rented her own apartment a few weeks into our relationship, because she needed a place to retreat to whenever she wanted. We would be together for the whole evening and suddenly she felt what she would describe as an “attack” and she had to leave. She was back the next day, happy and smiling as if nothing had happened. She was cycling back and forth on a ragged old bike and one day she took me to a used bike store and we found a fifty dollar bike for me. It never occurred to me that bicycles can be that cheap; to this day I’m not completely sure that store wasn’t dealing in stolen bikes, bicycle theft being a real widespread operation in Toronto.

We would go for bike rides by the lake shore, go visit her mom (I had my first bacon and eggs there, her mom was a fantastic cook), or visit her dad (they were divorced for many years).

During one of these visits, her dad told me the story behind Lara’s name: it turns out that she was born right after the movie Doctor Zhivago came out, and they were so touched by the beautiful love story and especially by the haunting “Lara’s Theme” piece that they decided to name their firstborn daughter Lara. I was flabbergasted because this was also my mom’s favorite movie, so much so that when she went to Venice with my dad they bought a small gondola souvenir with a music box that played that exact piece. I had played that music box so many times as a child that I ended up breaking the gondolier’s plastic oar. Later, my parents also obtained an original soundtrack LP from Russia that I also grew up listening to. When I told my mom the story, she was immediately pro-Lara, even before meeting her.

Both Lara’s parents were 50% Native American, making Lara half-native too. To me, she was Pocahontas: on a trip we took to visit some of her relatives in the Tyendinaga Territory, the chief offered to marry us right then and there on the reservation ground. Hearing this, I tried to persuade Lara to go for it, but she declined.

At some point I got an email from Dana, who was now working at Bell Labs with Alon Halevy, and living with her husband in a townhouse in New Jersey. She was inviting us to spend the winter vacation at her house: “You can go to the city anytime by train, we’re only half an hour away”, she said. When I told Lara, she was initially excited to go visit New York, but then she started worrying if she will be able to handle being together for that long, given her attacks. Up until we got into the train she was going back and forth, not sure whether she could go. Luckily the train pulled off just as she was about to get up and leave, so she stayed, but her face was breaking off. Too late to get off, so she endured the twelve hour ride somehow. We got off in Penn Station and took a local train to New Jersey. Dana and her husband Luis came to pick us up from the station with their car. Luis proved to be very good at putting Lara at ease with his conversation skills, and Lara was surprisingly good at making small talk. She had an entirely different personality when she was among strangers, just like playing a character in a play.

The next day we took the train to New York and spent the day walking around and filming with my brand new camcorder. I recorded our conversations where she was telling me that just half a pill of Rivotril was all it takes for her to love me. She was serious. We went to Central Park and sat on a bench and talked. She told me she would not be able to marry me, but that she’s happy to hang out for as long as I wanted, until I found someone to marry. “Even after”, she said, “I would move across the street from you and your wife and you can come and see me whenever you want”.

For the New Year’s party, we all went to New York, thinking we’ll see the ball drop in Time Square. Little did we know that people were camping there since the morning and the police had cordoned off the entire area. It was also bitter cold and Lara was wearing a black velvet dress, elegant, but not very warm. So cold that Lara and I spent most of the night in a nearby diner. Dana and Luis ventured on the streets but couldn’t get anywhere near Time Square so they eventually joined us. So did Alon and his wife. We all ended up celebrating the New Year in that dingy diner, still fun, but not worth the trip to the city, and especially the trip back, in an overcrowded train. We got to Dana’s place late that night, laughing at our own stupidity for not planning it better.

Our train back to Toronto was scheduled on January 3rd, at 8 in the morning. This time, we wanted to plan better so we decided to go the day before and stay with a friend of mine who had a small apartment midtown. His name was Bogdan, and he was a schoolmate of Jan’s (yes, that Jan), with an Aerospace degree from Bucharest, but now working as a software engineer in the city. We went together for dinner at the fancy Russian Samovar Restaurant and Piano Bar on 52nd street, and we got all mellow over the wine and live music. We lingered there until late at night and then we finally went to sleep around midnight. He generously offered us his bedroom while he slept on the couch in the living room. We set an alarm for the next morning and crashed. Predictably, we slept through the alarm and woke up less than an hour before the train’s scheduled departure from Penn Station. We scrambled to get ready and stormed out, in the freezing cold and hailed a cab. We told the driver our situation, and he beat all records and zoomed us through to the station barely in time to check in, but we made it! It was just like in one of the Seinfeld episodes, I kid you not.

Needless to say, the back-and-forth emotional roller coaster was exhausting. I had no choice, however; I was smitten with her. Many years later, when I watched a movie[^2] I identified with the main character.

When I met her, Lara had been a Teachers’ College student for a long time, taking just the minimum required credits every quarter so she would stay enrolled. In the fall of 1998, though, her program included a so-called “practicum”, a quarter spent in an elementary school, working as a teacher’s assistant. Lara, proud of her Native American heritage, chose an elementary school in Deseronto, Ontario, where most students were from the adjacent Tyendinaga Mohawk Territory. I remember how happy I was for her, even though that meant she would have to move there temporarily (Deseronto is about two hours East of Toronto, on Highway 401, just before reaching Kingston). This meant she was getting closer to graduating and becoming a teacher.

She found a house for rent in Deseronto, together with another girl in the same program. I rented a small truck and we loaded it with all of her possessions, except the grand piano, which she already had movers install in my living room, for safekeeping while she was away. It took us most of the day to load everything from her apartment on Spadina Avenue. Exhausted, we got into the truck and I drove her to Deseronto, all the while talking about her plans to graduate, and her eventual teaching career. Halfway through, we spotted a modern Service Center, and we stopped for coffee at Tim Hortons. After that, Lara would always say she wished we could go to a Service Center, even when we were back in the city. She loved acting like a child, and I loved that about her. She never took herself too seriously, or anything else for that matter.

We got there in the evening, during a heavy rain, only to find the house locked and the landlord missing. We drove to a phone booth and I called the landlord, putting my most professionally demanding tone, despite my accent: “This is unacceptable, we are here at the agreed time, and we cannot get into the house.” By the time we drove back to the house, he was there with the keys. I helped her unload everything, and just crashed next to her on the mattress.

Lara couldn’t sleep though, and before the sun rose the next morning she woke me up to complain about another attack and ask me to leave. I was still tired and barely awake, and in the moment I thought she was unfair and selfish, so I stormed out and got into the truck. Seeing how upset I got, Lara ran after me and stood in front of the truck, hugging the truck with both arms, to keep me from leaving. I got out and we hugged and cried for a long time. Eventually, after we both calmed down, I started the truck and drove back to Toronto. I caught the sun rising halfway through, it was an eerie experience for me, first time on the road so early.

I spent the next few weeks trying to adjust to her absence. She was calling me several times a day, she was struggling to get along with the main teacher. I was trying to encourage her to stick with her program, but she kept saying she was getting ready to quit. After a while she started asking me to borrow her dad’s car and come pick her up for the weekends. I did, happy to see her again, but then on Mondays she would sleep in and not go back, then on Tuesdays. She was sleeping at my place because she didn’t have her own apartment in the city anymore. It was sad watching her slowly slip away from her mandatory training. It wasn’t that she was lazy, she was just not in the best frame of mind to face the challenges of a teaching position. Eventually she accumulated too many absences and she had to drop out of the practicum. I helped her move her stuff out of Deseronto, and into storage. When I left for my summer internship in California I gave her the keys to my apartment so she could stay there for the summer.

I realized then that the childish, carefree attitude I loved about her was the very thing that was also making it hard for her to hold a job. The only commitment she was able to consistently make was to attend her twelve-step AA meetings. She had given up alcohol completely a few years before I met her but was still attending regularly, sitting in a circle and helping others overcome addiction.

On one of my trips to Romania, Lara gave me an authentic Native American dream catcher as a present for my mom. My mom liked it so much she hung it on the wall next to her bed, where it still sits to this day, years after her passing, charged with all of our mom’s dreams, as my sister tells everyone who asks. I remember during that trip Lara would call me constantly on the phone (she had found a really inexpensive international calling card). On one of these calls she shared that she was late, and asked me if she should go downstairs to Shoppers’ Drug-mart to buy a pregnancy test. I remember my mom’s excitement for the next fifteen minutes while we were waiting for the results. Soon after Lara called again, it had been a false alarm. Lara and I sighed a breath of relief, we were not exactly ready for the responsibility, but my mom was actually disappointed.
